# Drugs and cannabis

## Summary: cannabis rules have changed, other drugs remain strictly illegal

Thailand has some of the strictest drug laws in the region. Possessing, using, selling or importing drugs such as methamphetamine, MDMA, cocaine, heroin and ketamine can lead to arrest, heavy fines, prison and deportation.

Cannabis is a special case. In 2022, most of the cannabis plant was removed from the list of narcotics, and many cannabis shops opened, including in Phuket. Since then the government has tightened the rules again, moving towards medical use under control. The rules have changed several times and may change again. ## What the law says about drugs and cannabis

The Narcotics Code B.E. 2564 brings together Thailand's drug laws. It classifies drugs into categories and sets offences for possession, use, sale, production, import and export. Penalties depend on the drug, the amount and the purpose, and the specific sections and penalties are TO VERIFY. Being found with more than a set quantity may be treated as possession for sale; the quantities are TO VERIFY.

For cannabis, Ministry of Public Health notifications control how flowers and extracts can be sold and used. Recent changes have reportedly limited sales to medical purposes with a prescription or certificate, and smoking cannabis in public may be treated as a nuisance or an offence. Extracts with high THC content may still be controlled as narcotics. The current rules on who may buy, where it may be used, and the penalties are all TO VERIFY. Taking cannabis out of Thailand, or into many other countries, can be a serious crime.

## In practice in Phuket

Some cannabis shops in Phuket still operate, and some say they need a prescription or a medical form. A shop being open does not guarantee that every sale is legal under the current rules. Police may check people in nightlife areas, and urine tests can be used. Drugs offered on the street or at parties may be illegal, fake or dangerous.

## What you can do

- Do not buy, carry or use illegal drugs in Thailand.
- If you are thinking of using cannabis, check the current rules first. Never smoke it in public, near children or in a hotel room where it is banned. Never carry cannabis products across a border.
- If you are arrested, stay calm and ask for an interpreter. Contact your embassy or consulate, and don't sign anything you cannot read.
- If someone has taken something and is unwell, call an ambulance immediately.

Call 1669 for a medical emergency, the police on 191, or the Tourist Police on 1155.
