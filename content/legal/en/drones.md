# Flying a drone as a tourist

## Summary: drones need registration in Thailand

Many visitors want to film Phuket's beaches and islands from the air, but Thailand has strict rules for drones. In general, drones must be registered before they are flown. A camera drone may need to be registered with two authorities: the aviation regulator and the telecommunications regulator. Insurance is usually required, and flying is banned or restricted near airports, government buildings and other sensitive places. Phuket International Airport is on the island, so large areas are restricted. This page is a draft summary, not legal advice. Registration steps, limits and penalties are TO VERIFY.

## What the law says about drones

Drones are treated as aircraft under the Air Navigation Act B.E. 2497. The Civil Aviation Authority of Thailand (CAAT) issues rules on registering and operating unmanned aircraft. Separately, the National Broadcasting and Telecommunications Commission (NBTC) controls radio equipment, and drones that use radio frequencies may need to be registered with it.

Operating rules generally include:
- flying only in daylight and within sight;
- staying below a maximum height;
- keeping a minimum distance from people, buildings and airports;
- not flying over crowds or restricted areas without permission.

The exact limits, whether third-party insurance is required and at what level, and the penalties for flying an unregistered drone are TO VERIFY.

## In practice in Phuket

Registration for visitors is usually done online, and approval can take time, so plan before you travel. Customs may ask about drones in your luggage. The airport in the north of the island creates a large no-fly zone, and some national parks and islands have their own restrictions or need separate permission. Police and park officials may ask to see your registration and insurance, and may seize a drone that is flown illegally.

## What you can do

- Before travelling, check the latest registration rules with the aviation authority and the telecommunications regulator, and allow enough time for approval.
- Buy third-party insurance that covers drone use in Thailand.
- Carry your registration documents and insurance with you when flying.
- Use a drone map app to avoid airport zones and restricted areas, and ask park staff before flying in a national park.
- Never fly over crowds, beaches full of people or private property without permission.
- If an official stops you, stay calm, show your documents and ask for an official receipt for any fine.

For help, call the Tourist Police on 1155.
