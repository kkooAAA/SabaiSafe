# Smoking and littering on beaches

## Summary: many Phuket beaches are non-smoking

Several popular beaches in Thailand, including some in Phuket such as Patong, have been declared non-smoking areas. On those beaches, smoking is only allowed in marked smoking zones, usually set back from the sand. Throwing cigarette butts or other rubbish on the beach or in the sea can also be an offence.

These rules apply to visitors as well as residents, and officials do carry out checks. The list of beaches covered and the fines are TO VERIFY. This page is a draft summary, not legal advice.

## What the law says about smoking and litter

The Tobacco Products Control Act B.E. 2560 lets the authorities name public places as non-smoking areas, and makes it an offence to smoke in them. Under this framework, the authorities have named certain beaches as non-smoking areas, outside any designated smoking zone. Which Phuket beaches are covered, and the exact notice that covers them, are TO VERIFY. The penalties for smoking in a non-smoking area are TO VERIFY. Using e-cigarettes is a separate issue; see the vaping topic.

The Public Cleanliness and Orderliness Act B.E. 2535 makes it an offence to throw or leave rubbish in public places, which includes beaches. Local authorities may also have their own rules. The sections and fines are TO VERIFY. Coastal and marine protection rules may also apply to damage to the beach or the sea; these are TO VERIFY.

## In practice in Phuket

On beaches covered by the ban, you will usually see signs and marked smoking areas near the beach entrances or the road. Beach officers and local officials may warn smokers or issue fines, especially on busy beaches.

Cigarette butts are one of the most common types of litter found on Phuket beaches. Clean-up campaigns are common, and some beach vendors provide ashtrays only in permitted areas.

## What you can do

- Look for signs at the beach entrance, and only smoke in marked smoking areas.
- If you are unsure, assume a busy tourist beach is non-smoking.
- Carry a portable ashtray or a bag, and take all your rubbish with you.
- If an officer says you have broken a rule and asks you to pay a fine, ask to see their ID and ask for an official receipt.
- If you are asked for money in a way that seems unofficial, note the details and use the Report Assistant to prepare a Thai summary.

Call the Tourist Police on 1155 for help.
