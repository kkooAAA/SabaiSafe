# Taxi and tuk-tuk fares

## Summary: taxi and tuk-tuk fares in Phuket

Phuket has several kinds of paid transport. There are metered taxis, airport taxis and limousines with fixed fares, and unmetered "taxi" cars and tuk-tuks that charge by agreement or by zone. There are also motorbike taxis, songthaew (shared buses) and ride-hailing apps. Fare disputes are among the most common complaints from visitors.

The main rule of thumb is simple. Know which kind of vehicle you are in, and agree the price or confirm the meter before the trip starts. This page explains what the transport laws generally require, how fares work in Phuket, and what to do if a driver asks for more than you agreed. It is a draft summary, not legal advice. ## What the transport laws say about fares

Public passenger transport in Thailand is regulated mainly by the Land Transport Act B.E. 2522 and the Motor Vehicle Act B.E. 2522. Under these laws, a vehicle that carries paying passengers must be registered for that purpose, and its driver must hold a public driving licence. Officially registered taxis and tuk-tuks usually have yellow licence plates, and registered drivers often display an ID card inside the vehicle.

The authorities set the fares for metered taxis, and a metered taxi is generally expected to use its meter. For unmetered vehicles, the provincial land transport authorities may publish reference or maximum fares for set routes. ## How fares work in practice in Phuket

In Phuket, many cars marked "taxi" and nearly all tuk-tuks do not use a meter. Drivers usually quote a price per trip, and prices in tourist areas such as Patong can be high. Some taxi stands post a price board for common routes, which is a useful reference. Phuket Airport has official taxi and limousine counters with a fixed price for each destination.

Common problems include:
- the driver raising the price at the end of the trip;
- the driver claiming the meter is broken;
- the driver taking a longer route;
- the driver saying the agreed price was per person, not per trip.

## What you can do about a fare dispute

- Before the trip, ask for the meter, or agree the total price for the whole group and repeat it back. Write it down or show it on your phone.
- Note or photograph the licence plate and the driver's ID card if one is displayed.
- If you use a ride-hailing app, keep the in-app quote and receipt, and report problems through the app.
- If a driver asks for more than you agreed, stay calm and don't argue in a dangerous place. Pay what you agreed if it is safe to do so, and ask for help.
- Use the Report Assistant in this app to prepare a written Thai summary of what happened to bring to the police.

For help, call the Tourist Police on 1155. In an emergency, call the police on 191.
