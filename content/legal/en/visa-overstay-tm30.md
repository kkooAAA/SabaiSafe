# Visa overstay and TM30 address registration

## Summary: staying legally in Thailand

Every foreign visitor to Thailand is given a permitted period of stay when they enter, shown by the stamp or record in their passport. Staying beyond that date is an overstay. An overstay can lead to a fine, and a longer overstay can lead to detention, deportation and a ban on returning.

There is also a reporting system often called "TM30". The person who provides your accommodation must notify immigration where a foreigner is staying. Hotels usually do this for you. If you stay in a private home or a rented condo, it may not happen automatically. Entry and visa rules change often. This page is a draft summary, not legal advice.

## What the Immigration Act says

The Immigration Act B.E. 2522 governs entry, stay and departure of foreigners. A foreigner who stays beyond their permitted period commits an offence. Overstay fines are commonly charged per day, with a maximum amount. The daily rate and the maximum are TO VERIFY. A longer overstay, or being caught overstaying rather than reporting it yourself, may lead to a ban on re-entering Thailand for a period that depends on the length of the overstay. The ban periods are TO VERIFY.

The Act also requires the owner, manager or host of a place where a foreigner stays to notify immigration within a set time. This is the TM30 notification. The time limit, the exact section and the penalties are TO VERIFY. Separate rules require foreigners who stay a long time to report their address to immigration regularly; these are TO VERIFY.

## In practice in Phuket

Phuket Immigration Office handles extensions of stay, address reports and other immigration matters for the island. Hotels and licensed guesthouses normally make the TM30 notification themselves. If you rent a private condo or stay with friends, ask the owner or host whether they have notified immigration. You may need proof of the notification when you apply for an extension.

Overstay fines are usually paid at the airport or at an immigration office when you leave or report. Visitors who overstay because of illness or flight cancellations should go to immigration as early as possible.

## What you can do

- Check the permitted-stay date in your passport stamp or entry record as soon as you arrive.
- If you need more time, apply for an extension at Phuket Immigration Office before your permitted stay ends.
- If you stay in private accommodation, ask the host to make the TM30 notification, and keep a copy or a photo of the receipt.
- If you have overstayed, go to immigration or the airport immigration counter as soon as possible.
- If you are detained by immigration, ask for an interpreter and contact your embassy or consulate.

For help, call the Tourist Police on 1155.
