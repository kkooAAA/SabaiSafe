# Alcohol sale hours and drinking rules

## Summary: when and where alcohol can be sold

Thailand limits when, where and to whom alcohol can be sold. Visitors often notice this when a convenience store refuses to sell beer at certain times of day, or on Buddhist holidays and election days. The rules mainly target sellers, but some rules on drinking in certain places, and on drink-driving, apply to everyone. The rules have changed in recent years and may change again, so the exact hours below are TO VERIFY. This page is a draft summary, not legal advice.

## What the law says about selling and drinking alcohol

The Alcoholic Beverage Control Act B.E. 2551 and the official notices made under it control alcohol sales.

The rules generally include:
- limits on the hours of sale;
- no sales to people under the legal drinking age;
- no sales or drinking in certain places, such as temples, schools, petrol stations, many government buildings and public parks;
- limits on alcohol advertising.

The legal hours of sale have long been described as late morning to early afternoon, and from late afternoon to midnight. These hours have been under review, and the current exact hours are TO VERIFY. Sales are usually banned on certain Buddhist holidays and on election days; the dates change each year and are TO VERIFY. Recent changes may also make it an offence to drink in a shop or bar outside the permitted hours; this is TO VERIFY. Penalties are TO VERIFY.

Drink-driving is a separate offence under the Land Traffic Act B.E. 2522, with blood alcohol limits that are TO VERIFY.

## In practice in Phuket

Supermarkets and convenience stores in Phuket follow the sale hours strictly, and tills may block alcohol outside permitted times. Bars and nightlife areas such as Bangla Road in Patong may have different closing times under entertainment venue rules. Which zones may stay open later, and until when, is TO VERIFY. On alcohol-ban days, many bars close or stop serving alcohol, and police checks are common.

Police run drink-driving checkpoints, especially at night and during holidays.

## What you can do

- Check the date: on Buddhist holidays and election days, alcohol sales are usually banned across the country.
- Don't drink at temples, on public transport or in other restricted places.
- Never drive or ride a motorbike after drinking. Use a taxi, a ride-hailing app or a hotel shuttle.
- If a venue refuses to serve you because of the legal hours, accept it; staff can be fined.
- If you are hurt or in danger after a dispute at a bar, get to a safe place and call for help.

Call the Tourist Police on 1155. In an emergency, call the police on 191 or an ambulance on 1669.
