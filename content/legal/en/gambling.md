# Gambling

## Summary: most gambling is illegal in Thailand

Most forms of gambling are illegal in Thailand, including casinos, card games and betting for money, sports betting and online gambling. The main exceptions are the official government lottery and a few activities run with official permission. Both the organisers and the players can be arrested.

Visitors sometimes join card games in bars, betting on football, or games that seem friendly but are run as scams. There has been public debate about legalising casinos in entertainment complexes; the current position is TO VERIFY. This page is a draft summary, not legal advice.

## What the law says about gambling

The Gambling Act B.E. 2478 is the main law. It lists types of gambling that are banned outright and types that may only take place with an official licence. Playing, organising or providing a place for illegal gambling can be offences. Online gambling and betting through websites are generally treated as illegal gambling too.

The exact sections, the list of banned and licensable games, and the penalties for players and organisers are TO VERIFY. Proposals to allow licensed casinos within entertainment complexes have been discussed; whether any law has been passed and when it would take effect are TO VERIFY.

## In practice in Phuket

Police raid illegal gambling venues and online betting operations from time to time, and foreigners have been arrested in these raids. Card or dice games in bars, even for small amounts, can be illegal.

A common scam involves a friendly stranger who invites a tourist to a private home or room to play cards. The tourist is allowed to win at first, then loses a large amount and is pressured to pay, sometimes with threats. Selling lottery tickets above the printed price can also be an offence, for the seller.

## What you can do

- Don't take part in card games, betting or casino-style games for money, including online.
- Be careful with strangers who invite you to play cards or meet a "rich relative". Leave if you feel pressured.
- If you have been cheated or threatened in a gambling scam, go somewhere safe and keep any evidence, such as messages, names and locations.
- Use the Report Assistant to prepare a Thai summary for the police.
- If you are arrested, ask for an interpreter and contact your embassy or consulate. Don't sign documents you cannot read.

Call the Tourist Police on 1155. If you are threatened, call the police on 191.
