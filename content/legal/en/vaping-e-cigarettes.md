# Vaping and e-cigarettes

## Summary: e-cigarettes are banned in Thailand

Thailand bans the import and sale of e-cigarettes, vapes and refill liquids. This surprises many visitors, because vapes are openly sold in some tourist areas, but being on sale in a shop does not make them legal. Tourists have been fined or detained for having vapes, including at airports and at street checks. The law and how strictly it is enforced have changed several times, and they may change again. This topic is marked high priority for checking by our legal team. Treat everything below as a draft, not legal advice.

## What the law says about vapes

The ban rests on several rules, not one single vaping law.

- **Import:** a Ministry of Commerce notification bans importing e-cigarettes and their liquids into Thailand. Bringing them in can be treated as smuggling or as importing prohibited goods under the Customs Act B.E. 2560. Under the same Act, having or buying such goods knowing they were illegally imported can also be an offence.
- **Sale:** an order under the Consumer Protection Act B.E. 2522 bans selling e-cigarettes and providing vaping services.

The exact notices, sections and penalties are TO VERIFY. Penalties under customs law can be serious and may be linked to the value of the goods; the amounts are TO VERIFY. Whether new legislation has changed this position is TO VERIFY.

## In practice in Phuket

Vape shops and street stalls have appeared in some tourist areas, but police and officials have carried out crackdowns, especially recently. Tourists have reported being stopped and searched, having their vapes seized and being asked to pay fines. In some reported cases, demands for money did not follow official procedure. Airport customs may seize vapes found in luggage.

Using a vape where smoking is banned, such as on beaches with a smoking ban, can also break smoking rules.

## What you can do

- The safest choice is not to bring, buy or use vapes or e-liquids in Thailand.
- If you are stopped by police, stay calm and polite. Ask to see the officer's ID, and ask for an official receipt for any fine.
- Don't sign documents you cannot read. You can ask for an interpreter and contact your embassy or consulate.
- If you think a demand for money is not genuine, note the officer's name, the place and the time, and use the Report Assistant to prepare a Thai summary.

Call the Tourist Police on 1155 for help and advice.
