# Motorbike rental and passports held as deposit

## Summary: renting a motorbike in Phuket

Renting a scooter or motorbike is popular in Phuket, but it brings three common problems:
- riding without the right licence;
- accidents without proper insurance;
- damage disputes while the shop is holding your passport.

Many rental shops ask for a passport as a deposit. This gives the shop a lot of power in any dispute, because without your passport you cannot travel, check in at many hotels or leave the country. This page explains the general rules on licences, helmets and insurance, how the rental contract works, and what to do in a damage dispute. It is a draft summary, not legal advice.

## What the law says about licences, helmets and insurance

Under the Land Traffic Act B.E. 2522, anyone riding a motorbike on a public road must hold a valid licence for that type of vehicle. For visitors, this generally means either a Thai motorcycle licence, or a valid foreign licence together with an International Driving Permit that covers motorcycles. Whether a particular foreign licence is accepted is TO VERIFY. The rider and any passenger must wear a helmet. Penalties for these offences are TO VERIFY.

Every vehicle must have compulsory third-party insurance under the Motor Vehicle Accident Victims Protection Act B.E. 2535. This basic cover is limited. A rental shop may or may not offer extra insurance for damage to the bike. A travel insurance policy may refuse to pay if you were riding without a valid licence.

The rental itself is a hire of property under the Civil and Commercial Code. You are generally responsible for damage you cause, but not for damage that was already there. Whether a shop may legally keep a passport as a deposit is TO VERIFY by our legal team.

## In practice in Phuket

Police checkpoints for helmets and licences are common in tourist areas, and fines are often paid on the spot with a receipt. Many shops rent bikes without asking to see a licence, but that does not make it legal to ride without one.

Damage disputes usually start when you return the bike. The shop points to scratches and asks for money before handing back the passport. Photos taken when you pick up the bike are the best protection. Some shops accept a cash deposit or a copy of your passport instead of the original. The trusted businesses listed in this app that meet the "no passport deposit" criterion do not keep passports.

## What you can do

- Choose a shop that gives a written contract and does not keep your passport. Offer a cash deposit or a passport copy instead.
- When you pick up the bike, photograph and video it from all sides, including the fuel level and any existing scratches.
- Ask what insurance is included, and get it in writing.
- Always wear a helmet, and only ride if you hold the right licence.
- In a dispute, don't sign anything you cannot read. Ask the Tourist Police to help settle it.
- Use the Report Assistant to prepare a written Thai summary for the police.

Call the Tourist Police on 1155. After an accident with injuries, call 1669 for an ambulance and 191 for the police.
