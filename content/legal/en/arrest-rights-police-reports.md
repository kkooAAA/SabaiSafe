# Your rights if arrested and how to file a police report

## Summary: dealing with the police as a foreigner

Two situations bring visitors into contact with the Thai police: being a victim who needs to report a crime, and being suspected or arrested. In both cases, language is often the biggest problem, because official records and statements are written in Thai.

This page explains, in general terms:
- the rights a suspect has under the Criminal Procedure Code;
- how filing a police report works;
- why you should never sign a document you do not understand.

SabaiSafe's Report Assistant helps you prepare a Thai summary to bring to the police. It does not file a report for you. This page is a draft summary, not legal advice.

## What the Criminal Procedure Code says

The Criminal Procedure Code sets out how the police investigate offences and what rights suspects and victims have. In general, a person who is arrested has the right:
- to be told the charge;
- to contact a lawyer or someone they trust;
- to have an interpreter if they do not understand Thai;
- not to be forced to make a statement.

Foreign nationals may ask for their embassy or consulate to be informed. The time limits for bringing an arrested person before a court, and the rules on bail and temporary release, are TO VERIFY. The exact sections are TO VERIFY.

A victim can make a complaint, called a แจ้งความ, to the police. For certain offences, the complaint can lead to a criminal investigation. The police record the report in a daily record, and you can ask for a copy. Whether some offences can only be prosecuted if the victim files a complaint within a time limit is TO VERIFY.

## In practice in Phuket

Reports are usually made at the police station that covers the area where the incident happened. The Tourist Police can help visitors communicate with local stations and often act as a first point of contact. Bring your passport, any evidence, and if possible someone who speaks Thai.

Statements are taken in Thai. Read back carefully, or ask for a translation, before you sign. For insurance claims, ask for a copy of the police daily record.

## What you can do

- To report a crime, go to the local police station or contact the Tourist Police. Bring your passport, photos, receipts and any messages.
- Use the Report Assistant to prepare a clear Thai summary with an English copy before you go.
- Ask for a copy of the police daily record for insurance.
- If you are arrested, stay calm. Ask for an interpreter, a lawyer and for your embassy or consulate to be told.
- Don't sign anything you cannot read or do not agree with.

Call the Tourist Police on 1155. In an emergency, call the police on 191.
