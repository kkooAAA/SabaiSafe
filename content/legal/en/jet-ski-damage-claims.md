# Jet ski damage claims

## Summary: when a jet ski operator says you caused damage

The jet ski "damage scam" is a well-known problem on Phuket beaches, especially in Patong. After a ride, the operator points to scratches or cracks and asks for a large payment. Sometimes several people stand around the tourist, or the operator holds the tourist's passport. Some damage claims are genuine, but many involve damage that was there before the ride.

Thai law treats a jet ski rental as a contract between you and the operator. A real claim for damage is a civil matter, which should be settled fairly or decided by the proper authorities. Using threats, intimidation or your documents to force you to pay may be a crime. This page is a draft summary, not legal advice.

## What the law says about rental damage and threats

Renting a jet ski is a hire of property under the Civil and Commercial Code. In general, the person who hires property must take reasonable care of it and may be responsible for damage they cause. They are not responsible for damage that was already there or for normal wear. The person claiming money normally has to show what damage was caused and how much it costs to repair. The exact sections that apply are TO VERIFY.

Using threats or force to make someone pay can be a crime under the Criminal Code, for example extortion or intimidation. Which offence applies depends on the facts, and the sections and penalties are TO VERIFY. The Phuket authorities have also issued local rules for jet ski operators, covering things like registration, insurance and permitted zones. The details are TO VERIFY.

## How damage claims happen in practice in Phuket

Operators usually ask to be paid in cash on the spot. The amount can be many times the real repair cost. Some operators ask for your passport as a deposit before the ride, which makes it hard to walk away during a dispute.

The Tourist Police and local officials have helped settle many of these disputes. The result is often a lower payment or none at all, especially when the tourist has photos or video taken before the ride. Without that evidence, it is harder to show that the damage was already there.

## What you can do to protect yourself

- Before the ride, take clear photos and a slow video of the whole jet ski, including the edges, hull and seat. If possible, have the operator in the video.
- Ask whether insurance is included, and get a receipt with the business name.
- Don't hand over your passport. Offer a copy or another kind of deposit instead.
- If the operator claims a large sum, stay calm and don't sign anything you cannot read. Ask for the Tourist Police to come and help settle the dispute.
- Don't pay a large amount under pressure. If you feel unsafe, move to a public place and call for help.
- Use the Report Assistant in this app to prepare a written Thai summary for the police.

Call the Tourist Police on 1155. If you are threatened or in danger, call the police on 191.
