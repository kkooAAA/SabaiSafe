# Hotel and guesthouse deposits and damage claims

## Summary: deposits, check-out disputes and licensed accommodation

Hotels, guesthouses, villas and short-term condo rentals in Phuket often ask for a security deposit at check-in, in cash or as a credit card hold. Disputes happen when a deposit is not returned, or when a guest is charged for damage, cleaning or missing items at check-out. Visitors may also be asked to leave their passport as a deposit.

Thai law requires hotels to be licensed and to register their guests. Short-term rentals of private homes or condos may also be subject to rules. This page explains the general position on deposits and damage claims. It is a draft summary, not legal advice.

## What the law says about accommodation

The Hotel Act B.E. 2547 requires a business that provides paid accommodation to travellers to hold a hotel licence, except for small operations that may be exempt under its rules. Licensed hotels must register their guests, which is linked to the immigration reporting described in the visa topic. Whether renting out a private condo or house by the day or week requires a hotel licence is TO VERIFY. The sections and penalties are TO VERIFY.

The relationship between a guest and the accommodation is a contract under the Civil and Commercial Code. A deposit is generally security for the guest's obligations, and should be returned once those obligations are met. The host may deduct the real cost of damage the guest caused, but not normal wear or damage that was there before. The exact rules are TO VERIFY.

## In practice in Phuket

Most hotels return card holds or cash deposits at check-out after a room check. Disputes are more common with private villas and condos booked through websites or social media. Problems can include unclear house rules, large cleaning fees, or claims for damage that the guest says was already there.

Booking platforms usually have their own dispute and refund process, which may be faster than going to the authorities. For accommodation booked directly, keep all messages and receipts.

## What you can do

- Before paying a deposit, ask how much it is, what it covers and when it will be returned. Get a receipt.
- Avoid leaving your passport as a deposit. Offer a copy or a card hold instead.
- When you check in, photograph or video the room and note any existing damage. Send the photos to the host.
- At check-out, ask for the room to be checked in your presence, and get the deposit back or a written list of deductions.
- If a booking platform was used, open a dispute through the platform.
- If a dispute can't be resolved, contact the Consumer Protection Board or the Tourist Police. Use the Report Assistant to prepare a Thai summary.

Call the Consumer Protection Board on 1166 or the Tourist Police on 1155.
