# Beach safety flags, swimming and sunbeds

## Summary: swimming safely and beach rules in Phuket

Phuket's west coast beaches, such as Patong, Karon, Kata, Kamala and Surin, can have dangerous rip currents, especially during the southwest monsoon, roughly from May to October. Drownings happen every year, often when visitors swim on days when red flags are flying.

Beaches are also managed by local rules on sunbeds, umbrellas, vendors and water sports zones. This page explains the warning flags, the general beach rules and what to do in an emergency. It is a draft summary, not legal advice. Local orders change, and the details are TO VERIFY.

## What the rules say about flags and beach use

Lifeguard services on Phuket beaches commonly use these flags:
- **Red:** no swimming, because of dangerous conditions.
- **Red and yellow:** marks the area watched by lifeguards where swimming is allowed.
- **Yellow:** take care.

The exact flag system and whether ignoring a red flag can lead to a penalty are TO VERIFY.

Provincial and local authorities issue orders to organise beaches. These may cover:
- where sunbeds and umbrellas can be placed and how much of the beach they may use;
- the hours vendors may operate;
- zones for swimming and for jet skis or other water sports, often marked with buoys.

These orders are TO VERIFY. Rules on national park beaches and islands may be different.

## In practice in Phuket

Lifeguards are posted at major beaches during the day, but not everywhere and not at night. Rip currents are hard to see; they often look like a calm channel of darker water between the waves. Water sports operators should keep out of the swimming zones, but this is not always respected.

In recent years, local authorities have regulated sunbed and umbrella rental, and some beaches have sunbed-free areas where you can sit on your own towel. Rental prices may be set or displayed; see Fair Price for sample ranges.

## What you can do to stay safe

- Never swim when red flags are flying, and stay between the red and yellow flags when lifeguards are on duty.
- Don't swim alone, at night or after drinking alcohol.
- If you are caught in a rip current, don't swim straight back against it. Float, raise your arm for help, and swim parallel to the shore until you are out of the current.
- Keep children within arm's reach in the water.
- Stay out of buoyed water sports lanes when swimming.
- If someone is in trouble in the water, alert a lifeguard and call for help immediately.

Call 1669 for an ambulance, the police on 191, or the Tourist Police on 1155.
