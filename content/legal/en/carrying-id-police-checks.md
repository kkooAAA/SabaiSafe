# Carrying your passport and police ID checks

## Summary: should you carry your passport in Phuket?

Foreigners in Thailand may be asked by police or immigration officers to show their passport or other proof of identity and legal stay. Many visitors worry about losing their passport and prefer to leave it in the hotel safe. In practice, a clear copy of the photo page and of your entry stamp, or a photo of them on your phone, is often accepted for routine checks. But an officer may ask to see the original, and you may be asked to fetch it.

Police in Phuket also run checkpoints and street checks, for example for helmets, licences or drugs. This page explains your general obligations and rights during an ID check. It is a draft summary, not legal advice.

## What the law says about showing identification

Under the Immigration Act B.E. 2522, foreigners must have a valid passport and a lawful permission to stay, and immigration and police officers may check them. Whether you must carry the original passport at all times, or only produce it within a reasonable time when asked, is TO VERIFY. The penalties for failing to show identification are TO VERIFY.

Officers carrying out a check should identify themselves when asked. Any fine should be issued through official channels with a receipt. Asking for a bribe is an offence for the officer. The specific rules on searches and on your rights at a checkpoint are TO VERIFY. See also the topic on arrest rights and police reports.

## In practice in Phuket

Routine checks are common in tourist areas, especially at night, on main roads and in nightlife areas such as Patong. Officers may ask to see your passport, look at your motorbike licence, or in some cases ask for a urine test for drugs. Most checks are routine and short.

Visitors sometimes report people pretending to be police, or demands for cash without a receipt. Real Tourist Police officers wear uniforms and carry ID. If you are unsure, you can ask to go to the nearest police station, or call the Tourist Police to confirm.

## What you can do during an ID check

- Keep your passport in a safe place, and carry a copy of the photo page, your visa or entry stamp, and your entry card if you have one. A photo on your phone is useful too.
- Be ready to show the original passport if asked, or to fetch it from your hotel.
- Stay calm and polite. You may ask the officer for their name and ID.
- For any fine, ask for an official receipt.
- Don't sign documents you cannot read. Ask for an interpreter.
- If you think the person is not a real officer, or the demand is not genuine, call the Tourist Police and note the details.

Call the Tourist Police on 1155. In an emergency, call the police on 191.
