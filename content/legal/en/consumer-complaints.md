# Consumer complaints and refunds

## Summary: your rights as a consumer in Thailand

Foreign visitors are protected as consumers in Thailand in the same way as Thai buyers. Thai consumer protection law gives buyers a right to correct information about goods and services, to fair contract terms, and to complain when they are misled or treated unfairly. The main government body is the Office of the Consumer Protection Board (OCPB), known in Thai as สคบ.

You can complain about many things, for example:
- a tour that was not as advertised;
- a product that was misdescribed;
- a deposit that was not returned;
- an unfair contract term.

This page explains your basic rights and how to complain. It is a draft summary, not legal advice.

## What the consumer protection laws say

The Consumer Protection Act B.E. 2522 sets out consumers' basic rights. They include the right to receive correct and sufficient information about goods and services, the right to choose freely, and the right to compensation for damage. The Act also controls misleading advertising and unfair contract terms, and lets the Consumer Protection Board receive complaints and act on them. The exact sections are TO VERIFY.

The Consumer Case Procedure Act B.E. 2551 provides a simpler court procedure for consumer disputes. In general it lets a consumer bring a case without complex paperwork, and puts more of the burden of proof on the business. How it applies to short-stay visitors, and any time limits, are TO VERIFY.

## How complaints work in practice in Phuket

For small disputes, the quickest result usually comes from talking to the business directly, calmly and with evidence. If that fails, the Tourist Police can often help settle disputes between tourists and businesses on the spot. For a formal complaint, you can contact the Consumer Protection Board, which has a national hotline and works with provincial officials in Phuket.

Complaints are easier to handle when you have:
- receipts;
- screenshots of the advertisement or booking;
- photos;
- the business name and address.

If you leave Thailand before the matter is resolved, ask how you can follow up from abroad. That process is TO VERIFY.

## What you can do to complain

- Keep every receipt, booking confirmation, chat message and advertisement.
- Write down the date, time, place, the business name, and what was promised and what happened.
- Ask the business for a refund or a fix first, and note its answer.
- If that fails, contact the Consumer Protection Board, or ask the Tourist Police to help settle the dispute.
- Use the Report Assistant in this app to turn your facts into a clear Thai summary you can hand to officials.

Call the Consumer Protection Board on 1166, or the Tourist Police on 1155.
